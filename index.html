The Life and the Record

Every automated system works from a record. A record is a translation of a life, and translations lose things. Watch one person's life pass through a scoring system, see what falls away at each step, then change the design and see what it takes to keep it.

No background needed. Terms are explained where they appear. Ungraded, private, and fictional throughout.

      What information counts?

      Automated systems do not simply “look at everything.” People decide what information enters the system and how much influence different information has.

      Plain language: A weight is simply how much influence one piece of information has on a result. A threshold is the cutoff where the system changes what happens next.

      Each person's score = (their first number × its weight) + (their second number × its weight). Anyone at or above the cutoff gets flagged.

      Optional reflection: What is one piece of information about the people you serve that should never become a number in a system like this? Why that one?

      Practice Scan connection: this is the "Choice, privacy, and data" question, what information is used, and who decided it counts?

        Whose data is this?

        The CARE principles (Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics) were written for Indigenous data governance. Where data come from a Tribal or Indigenous community, that community's authority over its data is the starting point. This lab borrows the four questions to look at a fictional request.

        The request

        A county behavioral health office asks Cedar Bridge Wellness Circle, a fictional community-run peer program, for three years of attendance and check-in records. The county wants to train a tool that flags members "likely to disengage." The program's staff are deciding what to say. For each principle, choose the county's proposed terms, then see what each one leaves open.

        Week 8 companion

        Practice Scan Starter

        Use this before you write your Trauma-Informed Technology Practice Scan. Name the system in general terms, then mark where you stand on each of the eight questions. The page builds an outline that sorts what you know from what you still need to find out. Use a workflow, a public document, or a case. Leave out names of people and agencies.

        1. Setting and purpose

        Who uses the system, who is affected by it, and what problem is it meant to address?

        2. What the system does

        Is it rules-based, predictive, generative, or mostly administrative? Where does a person's judgment come in?

        3. Who benefits and who carries risk

        What gets easier or faster? Who may face new errors, burden, surveillance, or exclusion?

        4. Choice, privacy, and data

        What information does it use? Do people know it is involved? Can they decline or choose another route?

        5. Relationship, access, and culture

        What changes between people? Whose language, disability, family structure, or culture could it miss?

        6. Human review and correction

        Who can question, correct, or override the result? What happens when it is wrong?

        7. Workforce and cost

        Does it save time, move work elsewhere, add monitoring, or create new costs?

        8. Recommendation and alternatives

        What should continue, change, pause, or end? What else could address the same problem?

        Nothing is saved or sent. The outline is a planning aid and the assignment is graded on your own analysis.
